On 14 July 1518 a woman in Strasbourg started dancing in the street, with no music, and could not stop. Within weeks dozens, maybe hundreds, of people had joined her. The city council's cure: build dance stages and hire musicians. Archie reads the chronicles to find out what really happened, why it spread, and why the "15 deaths a day" figure is shakier than the internet thinks.

DOCUMENTED vs LEGEND
Documented: mass dancing in Strasbourg from mid-July to about early September 1518; the council opened guild halls, built stages in the horse and grain markets and hired musicians, then banned public dancing and most music; afflicted dancers were taken to the shrine of St Vitus at Saverne; earlier dancing outbreaks along the Rhine and Moselle since 1374.
Disputed: the number of dancers (estimates ~50 to ~400); the claim of up to 15 deaths a day comes from later accounts, and contemporary city records do not mention deaths.
Legend / opinion: that ergot poisoning caused it (most historians reject this); Paracelsus's claim that Frau Troffea did it to annoy her husband.
