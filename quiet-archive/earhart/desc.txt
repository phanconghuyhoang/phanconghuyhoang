On 2 July 1937 Amelia Earhart and navigator Fred Noonan took off from Lae, New Guinea, for tiny Howland Island, 2,556 miles away. The Coast Guard cutter Itasca could hear her radio calls, but she could not hear the ship. Her last message placed her on the "157-337" line. Archie follows the radio logs and tests the three main theories against the evidence.

DOCUMENTED vs LEGEND
Documented: Lockheed Electra 10E; departure from Lae 2 July 1937 about 10:00 local; Itasca heard but could not reach her; 7:42 a.m. "We must be on you but cannot see you... gas is running low"; last call on the 157-337 line; search ended 19 July 1937 at a cost of about $4 million; declared dead January 1939; partial skeleton found on Gardner Island (Nikumaroro) in 1940 and later lost; the 2017 "Marshall Islands photo" appeared in a 1935 book.
Legend: spy missions, a secret new identity, and any claim that the case is closed.
