On 30 June 1908 something exploded in the sky over central Siberia and flattened about 2,150 square kilometres of forest. A man 65 km away was thrown off his porch, instruments as far away as Britain recorded the blast, and the skies over Europe glowed for nights. When scientists finally reached the site in 1927, there was no crater. Archie follows the eyewitnesses, Leonid Kulik's expeditions and the evidence, and checks which theories survive, from asteroid airburst to Tesla's "death ray".

DOCUMENTED vs LEGEND
Documented: an airburst over the Podkamennaya Tunguska river on 30 June 1908 that flattened about 2,150 km² of forest; pressure waves recorded far away; bright night skies over Europe and western Russia; eyewitness S. Semenov thrown down at Vanavara, about 65 km away; at least three deaths reported; no confirmed crater.
Disputed: the energy (estimates about 3 to 30 megatons), the size and type of the object, and whether Lake Cheko is an impact crater.
Legend: a Tesla "death ray", an alien ship, a micro black hole, and the exact figure of "80 million trees".
