## s13 | mc/bg/board.png#fit | story
{a:hands_hips/neutral}{t:WHAT KILLED THEM?} So why did a well-supplied, modern expedition fail so completely? There are several suspects.
{a:point_l/neutral}{s:pop} Suspect one, the cold, the darkness, and the ice. Two summers in a row, the ice didn't melt enough to free the ships. That alone is a death sentence in slow motion.
{a:point_l/neutral}{s:pop} Suspect two, scurvy. Without enough fresh food, vitamin C runs out, and wounds stop healing, teeth loosen, and people get weak and confused.
{a:point_l/neutral}{s:pop} Suspect three, the tins. Remember them?

## s14 | franklin/bg/lab.png | story
{cam:0.5,0.5,1.0,1.04,0,0}{c:sci=sailor@0.35,0.88,0.05}{c:sci think/suspicious}{t:1984} In nineteen eighty four, a team led by anthropologist Owen Beattie carefully exhumed the three sailors buried on Beechey Island.
{a:lean_in/shocked} Thanks to the permafrost, the bodies were astonishingly well preserved.

{a:think/neutral} But later studies pushed back. Some found that lead levels were not that unusual for people of the time, and pointed instead to malnutrition, tuberculosis, and other illnesses.
{a:shrug/neutral} The honest answer is probably all of the above. Lead didn't help. Scurvy didn't help. Starvation and cold finished the job.
