On 26 May 1828 a teenage boy appeared in Nuremberg holding a letter, barely able to speak, repeating that he wanted to be a cavalryman like his father. He said he had spent his whole life in a dark cell. Within five years he became a European sensation, was rumoured to be a stolen prince of Baden, and died of a stab wound in Ansbach. Archie follows the records, the doubts and the DNA tests, including the 2024 study that finally tested the prince theory.

DOCUMENTED vs LEGEND
Documented: Hauser appeared in Nuremberg in May 1828 with two notes; he was cared for by Georg Friedrich Daumer and later sponsored by Lord Stanhope; he was found wounded in 1829 and hurt by a pistol in 1830; he died of a stab wound in Ansbach in December 1833, and a note in mirror writing was found; a 2024 mitochondrial DNA study (Walther Parson et al., iScience) found no match with the House of Baden.
Disputed: his account of a childhood in a dark cell; whether the attacks were real or self-inflicted; whether his death was murder, suicide or an accident.
Legend: that he was the stolen hereditary prince of Baden; that he could see in the dark.
